The Reborn Of Memories

Game Overview

The Reborn of Memories is a narrative-focused tabletop role-playing game where 4–5 players step into the roles of people from Oli’s life—family, friends, lovers and even a former bully—working together to reconstruct her fading memories. Through exploration, improvisation and contested dice rolls, players help Oli reclaim key moments from her past while deciding whose version of events becomes “true”.

Game Concept

  • Emotional fantasy: players become the most important figures in Oli’s life, trying to preserve or reshape how she remembers them.
  • Collaborative storytelling: everyone contributes details about people, places and events, using objects found during play as prompts for each memory scene.
  • Competitive spotlight: only one player can successfully “lock in” a memory each round, creating tension over who gets remembered and how.
  • Soft safety & agency: the limited-use “Just Say No” card lets players veto another memory if it conflicts with how they want Oli to remember them.

Core Play Loop

  1. Set the environment: the GM introduces the current setting (e.g. Serene Forest, Laboratory, Oli’s home) and frames the situation.
  2. Define memory goals: each player writes at least three goals describing the memories they hope Oli will regain in this setting.
  3. Explore for objects: players declare how they search the environment and roll a d20 against the GM to see if they discover a unique object. Successful finds are recorded and cannot be duplicated by other players.
  4. Trigger a memory phase: once enough objects have been gathered, the GM calls for a memory scene and chooses which character stat will be used this round.
  5. Regain memories: everyone describes a memory for Oli based on the objects they found, then rolls a d20 plus the chosen stat’s modifier. The highest total wins and that player’s memory becomes canon.
  6. Repeat in new locations: the group moves between map locations, pursuing fresh goals until Oli’s story feels complete.

Player Roles & Character Creation

Each player picks an archetype from Oli’s life—such as mother, classmate, boyfriend, ex-boyfriend or bully—and builds a custom character with their own personality, items and memory goals.

Stats & Attribute Economy
  • Stat pool: players distribute up to 12 points across four stats, with each stat rated from 1–5.
  • Memory Influence: how strongly the character can shape Oli’s memories.
  • Emotional Impact: the emotional weight their presence has on Oli.
  • Relationship Strength: how close they are to Oli.
  • Knowledge Depth: how much they know about Oli’s life events.

Higher investment in a stat improves that player’s odds whenever the GM chooses that stat for a memory scene, creating different “builds” such as emotionally-driven characters vs. knowledge-focused ones.

Item & Object Systems
  • Starting items: players freely choose any number of tools (e.g. rope, flashlight, notebook, shovel) from a shared item sheet to support their exploration.
  • Exploration objects: unique objects discovered via d20 checks become narrative currency. They are recorded on the character sheet and can be spent as prompts when framing memories with Oli.
  • No duplicates: once an object is found by one player, it is no longer available to others, pushing players to carve out different narrative niches.
Resolution Mechanics
  • Exploration Resolution: roll d20 vs the GM’s d20 to see if an object is discovered; success unlocks new narrative resources.
  • Memory Resolution: in a memory phase, all players roll d20 plus the chosen stat modifier; the highest total wins the right to define the memory.
  • “Just Say No” Card: each player receives three cards for the entire game. To cancel another player’s successful memory, they roll 2d6; on a success the targeted memory is erased and cannot be claimed, and the card is discarded.

GM Tools & Scenario Structure

The GM uses a simple node-based map (Laboratory, Library, Serene Forest, Chamber, Cafe, Art Studio, Oli’s Home) to pace the story and decide which environments Oli visits next. For each scene, the GM:

  • Frames the emotional context and stakes of the location.
  • Chooses which stat is relevant for the upcoming memory resolution.
  • Tracks discovered objects and monitors when the group has enough to trigger a memory phase.
  • Encourages players to reflect Oli’s personality and past while leaving space for new interpretations.

Design Goals & Takeaways

  • Emotion-first design: Mechanics are lightweight, pushing most decisions into collaborative narration rather than strict tactical play.
  • Shared authorship with friction: Everyone is trying to help Oli, but limited memory slots and “Just Say No” cards add drama over whose truth is preserved.
  • Replayable scenarios: Flexible character creation, open-ended goals and a modular map make it easy for the GM to reframe Oli’s life across multiple campaigns.
