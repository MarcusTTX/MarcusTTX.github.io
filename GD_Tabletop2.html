Day Break

Game Concept

DayBreak is a tactical, scenario-driven tabletop game set in a near-future walled city struggling to survive after a devastating conflict. The Eclipse Corporation controls the last safe zones, while insurgent forces, criminal factions, and internal conspiracies threaten to plunge what remains of civilization into chaos.

Players command a small squad of operatives navigating tight, cover-heavy urban maps. Each scenario presents a different tactical puzzle: infiltrate hostile compounds, rescue key targets, secure intel, or hold the line against escalating threats. Outcomes feed into a branching campaign, where failures and successes shift the state of the city and unlock new missions.

Game Overview

  • Genre: Tactical skirmish / campaign-based tabletop game
  • Focus: Squad positioning, line-of-sight, and coordinated abilities
  • Structure: Linked scenarios with branching narrative outcomes
  • Core Fantasy: Lead an elite DayBreak team in high-risk operations that decide the fate of the last surviving city.
  • Role: You handled system design, the GDD, and game balance across scenarios and unit kits.

Core Gameplay Loop

The experience is built around a tight tactical loop:

  1. Briefing: Read the scenario, objectives, and any special rules or events affecting the mission.
  2. Squad Preparation: Choose operatives, assign loadouts and consumables, and review the tactical map.
  3. Engagement: Take alternating turns with the enemy, spending actions to move, shoot, use abilities, and interact with mission objectives.
  4. Resolution: Check win / fail conditions, record casualties, rewards, and narrative choices.
  5. Campaign Progression: Unlock follow-up scenarios, adjust difficulty and stakes based on previous results.

Systems & GDD Summary

1. Unit & Role Design

The GDD defines several unit types (e.g. assault, support, recon, heavy) using a shared base unit template. Each template specifies stat baselines, available weapon classes, and upgrade tracks. Roles are designed to create clear tactical identities and synergies so squads must combine movement, suppression, and utility rather than relying on raw damage alone.

  • Unit Roles: Frontliners, ranged damage, utility / control, and specialists for scenario objectives.
  • Abilities: Defined as discrete actions with cost, range, target type, and effect; tuned for combo potential and counterplay.
  • Progression: Persistent bonuses or unlocks between missions (e.g. improved stats, new abilities, or upgraded equipment) as outlined in the Unit Progression section of the GDD.

2. Stat System

Units track a small set of primary stats (survivability, offensive power, and mobility) and secondary stats (accuracy modifiers, armor penetration, evasion, etc.). The rulebook separates these to keep turn-to-turn calculations fast while still allowing expressive build differences and balance tuning at the system level.

3. Action Economy & Turn Structure

A round is divided into a Player Phase, Enemy Phase, and End Phase. During their phase, players spend a limited number of actions per unit to:

  • Move across tiles and into / out of cover
  • Attack, suppress, or use special abilities
  • Interact with objectives (hack terminals, secure crates, escort VIPs, etc.)

The GDD formalizes action costs and priority rules, ensuring that line-of-sight, overwatch, and reactive abilities resolve in a predictable order and are easy to reference mid-game.

4. Combat Resolution

Combat is grid-based with strict enforcement of range bands, line of sight, and cover. The combat section of the rulebook breaks resolution into:

  • Targeting & Line of Sight: Rules for checking visibility around corners, elevation, and obstacles.
  • Attack Roll: A single resolution check to determine hit, miss, or critical result, modified by stats and cover.
  • Damage & Mitigation: Damage values, armor or damage reduction, and on-hit effects like suppression or status conditions.

These rules are tuned in your GDD for low ambiguity and quick reference so players can stay focused on tactical choices, not rule lookups.

5. Movement & Cover System

Movement uses a discrete cost system so players can quickly count legal paths and evaluate risk. The GDD specifies:

  • Base movement allowance per unit and how terrain modifies cost
  • Entering, leaving, and benefiting from partial / full cover
  • Rules for vaulting, jumping gaps, or moving through special tiles

This system is tightly integrated with the level layouts to promote flanking, cross-fires, and positional play rather than static trading.

6. Currency & Consumables

Missions reward a simple currency system that players spend on consumables and long-term upgrades. The Consumables section defines items such as grenades, healing kits, and temporary buffs, each with:

  • Clear action cost and timing (instant, end-of-round, reactive, etc.)
  • Area of effect / targeting rules
  • Trade-offs in power, reliability, and resource cost

7. Enemy AI & Behaviour

Enemy forces are driven by a structured AI flow chart:

  • Awareness States: Idle, alerted, engaged, and searching.
  • Priority Targets: How enemies choose between exposed, low-health, or high-threat units.
  • Behaviour Patterns: Patrol routes, defensive holds, reinforcements, and scenario-specific behaviours.

This system keeps enemy turns deterministic enough for tabletop play, while still creating pressure and forcing the squad to reposition proactively.

Scenario & Campaign Design

DayBreak is built around a growing library of scenarios, starting with Scenario 1: “Dirty Deeds”. Each scenario specifies:

  • Backstory & stakes in the broader DayBreak narrative
  • Primary and optional objectives with clear success / failure conditions
  • Map layout: key lanes, choke points, and objective locations
  • Enemy composition & deployment: patrols, reinforcements, and special units
  • Special rules: environmental hazards, timers, or stealth constraints when applicable

The campaign uses branching story nodes. Completing a mission, failing it, or choosing a particular extraction / moral option can unlock different follow-up scenarios and modify future enemy forces or city conditions. This gives DayBreak replayability and emphasises strategic planning beyond a single battle.

Design Responsibilities

My Responsibilities:

  • System Design: Defined the core combat, movement, currency, and AI systems so they work cleanly on the tabletop.
  • GDD Authoring: Structured the rulebook and design document into sections (stats, combat, movement, AI, scenarios) with diagrams, tables, and examples for future iteration.
  • Game Balance: Tuned unit stats, ability numbers, and scenario difficulty across multiple playtest passes to keep missions tense but fair.
